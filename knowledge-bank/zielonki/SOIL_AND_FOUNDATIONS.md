## Planning facts

- Groundwater was encountered at 1.6 m and 2.3 m below ground and may fluctuate seasonally.
- Near-surface silty clays are sensitive to moisture change, disturbance and dynamic loading.
- Peat was recorded with approximately 40–60% organic matter; organic mud with approximately 8–15%.
- Neither 3.5 m borehole penetrated the base of the weak organic layer.
- The report records geotechnical category I in complex ground conditions for the assumed house.
- No adverse mass movement or other geodynamic process was observed in the investigated area.

The later specialist interpretation reports weak-bearing soil to roughly 4.0 m and gives micropiles bearing in gravel at a minimum depth of about 5.5 m as the current concept. Geological-engineering documentation will probably be required for an indirect-foundation solution. This is not a final foundation design.

## Garden implications

- The demo conservatively models the root zone as clay-like with slow drainage.
- Soil pH is unknown because no pH test was found.
- Moisture-tolerant planting is the safe starting palette.
- Dry-climate plants require a raised, engineered and freely drained root zone.
- Do not place infiltration or retention beside foundations until groundwater and drainage are reviewed.
- Borehole markers in 3D are map-derived orientation aids, not setting-out points.

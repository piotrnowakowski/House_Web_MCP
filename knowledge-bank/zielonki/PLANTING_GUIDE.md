# Planting guide

This is a conservative concept palette for the current Zielonki model: cold winters, warm summers, heavy slow-draining ground, shallow groundwater and no measured pH. It is not a horticultural survey.

## Best-fit starting palette

| Plant | Botanical name | Best use | Why it fits | Main caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| European hornbeam | `Carpinus betulus` | Boundary hedge and screens | Hardy structure with tolerance of heavier, periodically moist soil | Check mature root spread and boundary offsets |
| Guelder rose | `Viburnum opulus` | Mixed hedge and moist shrub border | Hardy, seasonal flower and fruit, suitable for a moist palette | Avoid permanent standing water |
| Common dogwood | `Cornus sanguinea` | Naturalistic boundary groups | Robust shrub for heavier ground with winter and habitat value | Can spread |
| Downy birch | `Betula pubescens` | Distant canopy or agricultural edge | Better aligned with cool, periodically moist ground than silver birch | Keep well away from house, drains and utilities |
| Meadowsweet | `Filipendula ulmaria` | Moist meadow or rain-garden shoulder | Naturally suited to consistently moist planting | Not for dry raised beds |
| Tufted sedge | `Carex elata` | Reviewed swale or rain-garden edge | Strong matrix plant for periodically wet ground | Water-feature position needs drainage review |

## Conditional choices

- Apple, `Malus domestica`: retain the existing tree and place new orchard trees only in sunny, locally raised and improved root zones with verified drainage.
- Bigroot cranesbill, `Geranium macrorrhizum`: useful lower-water groundcover in a raised or moderately drained border; avoid saturated pockets.
- Hydrangea, `Hydrangea paniculata`: usable in a moist but aerated root zone; do not equate shallow groundwater with healthy drainage.
- Lavender, rosemary and similar dry Mediterranean species: only in raised, sharply drained beds. They are not default choices for the native soil model.

## Placement rules

1. Keep large moisture-seeking trees away from foundations, drains, services and the future foundation system.
2. Keep rain gardens and swales away from the house until a drainage specialist reviews groundwater and discharge.
3. Use hedges and shrubs as wind-filtering layers rather than one impermeable wall.
4. Reserve the sunniest, best-drained area for orchard and vegetable production.
5. Confirm soil pH, actual topsoil structure, seasonal saturation and light before ordering plants.

The machine-readable version of this palette lives in [`data.ts`](data.ts) and is returned through WebMCP as part of `get_project_state({ detail: "site" })` and the garden detail response.
